Swim

Athletics

Swimming taught how to fuel a body for the work, and that speed comes from the swimming, not from how a body looks.

The races are individual. The result is a team. A teammate’s best time counts, and a relay makes that obvious.

Leading well means every swimmer belongs, not only the fast ones, and the example is the work. It also means stepping back when staying would hurt the team.

Anyone faster is worth learning from, including swimmers who are younger. The skill that decides a practice is mental: stay with the discomfort instead of backing off. Meets are that same choice under a clock.

The day has to be scheduled tightly enough for practice, school, and sleep. The pool is a place to be odd and to own it. Deep water, and what is still unknown, is not a reason to stay on the wall. A crowded lane can still be quiet if the breath stays steady. Strength is worth keeping after the last race, and so is rest.
