About me

I turn ideas into physical things — CNC-machined robot parts for FRC, a wearable blood-pressure sensor for the Conrad Challenge, PVC wheelchair attachments for power soccer players who couldn’t afford specialized chairs. If it can be designed and built, I want to be the one building it.

That same instinct pulls me toward open questions: what’s next for commercial supersonic flight, and what two-dimensional semiconductors could do that today’s materials can’t. I like the discipline of research as much as the thrill of a working prototype — the writing, the revising, being told by an editor exactly what doesn’t hold up yet.

I’ve also run my own small business since 2023, sourcing and shipping scientific equipment, which taught me that a good idea means nothing without logistics, customer trust, and follow-through.

Engineering, to me, is where those three things meet — the discipline to build something real, the curiosity to ask what’s next, and the follow-through to see it land. I’m a high school senior applying to study electrical and computer, mechanical, or chemical engineering, especially where they meet aerospace, robotics, and computational work.
